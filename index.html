MEEN 305 / STRUCTURAL ANALYSIS

Less material.
More engineering.

Structural beam optimization & experimental validation

20team design iterations
≥1.5design factor of safety
~5%reported weight error

The challenge: minimize the weight of a 9-inch PLA beam spanning an 8-inch gap, while designing for two loading conditions and a minimum factor of safety of 1.5.

My contribution

I developed iterations 16–20, created CAD for iteration 18, refined the Excel optimization workflow, and supported simulations and the experimental test setup.

The result—and the lesson

The team’s selected hollow-square design used varying wall thickness. Its measured weight was close to the prediction, while deflection differed substantially. Testing made the limits of idealized supports, material assumptions, and manufacturing precision tangible.

MEEN 210 / MECHANICAL DESIGN

Making motion
mechanical.

From a laser-cut concept to a 3D-printed mechanism

01 / Laser-cut prototype02 / SolidWorks redesign03 / 3D fabrication

A team project that started as a flat wooden running figure and evolved into a three-dimensional assembly. The redesign replaced the main four-bar drive with a three-gear train and hand crank.

My contribution

I prepared the bill of materials and modeled the right arm, lower right leg, upper right leg, and right arm link, with their respective SolidWorks drawings. I also participated in assembly, fabrication, and evaluation of the mechanism.

Beyond the CAD model

Pinned joints and four- and five-bar linkages coordinate the figure’s motion. Real assembly brought fit, friction, gear alignment, and joint clearance into focus—small details that decide whether a mechanism moves freely.

SolidWorksTechnical drawingsLinkages & gears3D printing
Explore the design evolution +

The original 2D assembly used two four-bar linkages and one five-bar linkage, with 11 joints and 40 total parts. The 3D redesign introduced depth, revised mounting features, and a three-gear crank drive.

Fabrication required adjusting fit and reaming tight holes. The key lesson: successful CAD motion still depends on tolerances, alignment, friction, and an assembly strategy.
